J 0754 — CONSOLIDATED EVIDENCE
11 November 2026

J 0754 is a giant with reproducible multiyear fading, a recurring roughly 36-day photometric signal and evidence of magnetic activity. A near-synchronized active binary with evolving spots is the leading working explanation. The Gaia orbit remains a candidate; the data do not uniquely assign the flares/X-rays to a component or prove that spots cause the entire long fading.

The strongest measured results are repeat fading/recovery, independent optical replication, wavelength-dependent dimming, a recurring roughly 36-day signal, published chromospheric emission and archival X-rays.
The four TESS excursions remain positive under common measurements. Their conditional face-on energies are about 2.0, 2.5, 7.1,2.1e37 erg. November 16/November 28 have broad baseline envelopes. Apparent hourly position drift in November 22/December 23 remains an open attribution diagnostic; the tested pointing terms do not remove the brightness excess.
Gaia gives the companion candidate. Activity detections are not independent orbit confirmations. Spots fit the color changes, but nonuniform obscuration survives. Radio images add limits; UVOT exposure edges prevent a flare inference; GALEX timing supplies only a weak variability hint.
Next decisive measurements: calibrated high-resolution RV/activity spectra, simultaneous multicolor/temperature measurements, and the remaining TESS astrometry checks. Other-star screening is the next separately scheduled stage.
