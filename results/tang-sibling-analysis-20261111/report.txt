TANG'S OTHER TWO HISTORICAL FADING GIANTS: MODERN LIGHT-CURVE ANALYSIS
11 November 2026 | Kyle Hicks and Ari | archival, exploratory analysis

FINDING

J0830 shows a strong 38–39-day brightness modulation in 2021 TESS data, close
to its 38.389-day Gaia candidate orbital period. The fitted variation is about
5.2–5.4% from minimum to maximum under the simpler reference-offset models.
J0736 favors a shorter 19–21-day signal; a stable 39.421-day waveform is less
successful. Neither star yields a qualifying positive excursion in the initial
seven-file flare screen. J0830 is the stronger lead for the activity/binary
comparison with J0754.

Each Gaia solution uses 12 accepted radial-velocity measurements. These are
catalogue candidates, not orbits newly fitted or confirmed in this analysis.
The prior screening retains the original Gaia responses and formal errors.

Both targets have QLP TESS sectors 44, 45 and 46 from 2021. After QUALITY=0,
finite-time and positive-finite-flux cuts, the primary aperture flux contains
8,416 observations for J0736 and 8,562 for J0830, reduced to 329 and 328
distinct 0.2-day bins. Their spans are 78.12 and 77.95 days. J0830 also has
K2 campaign 5 observations from 2015, EPIC 211569759: 2,160 strict accepted
SAP points, 283 bins and a 74.78-day span. No brightness clipping is applied.
FITS identifiers and positions match the intended catalogue targets within
one arcsecond. This verifies product identity; pixel-level host attribution
is a separate task.

The seven previously retained FITS are unchanged. One additional K2SC FITS
was recovered for the same 2015 exposures. An EVEREST response was available
but exceeded the retrieval script's 12 MB retention bound; it was not saved
or analyzed. That is a size-policy failure, not an archive nondetection.

METHOD

Use aperture SAP flux for slow variability. QLP's KSPSAP products are filtered
for transit searches and remove much of the multi-week structure. Their small
remaining amplitudes cannot constrain the original stellar signal. Normalize
each sector by its accepted median, then jointly fit calibration terms and
sine/cosine terms. Search 5–100 days on 1,601 uniformly spaced frequencies;
test each Gaia period, its half-period and a two-harmonic representation.
Compare global, sector-linear, quadratic and centroid/background models.
The two epochs are fitted separately, with no phase coherence across six years.

An independent review identified a useful pipeline-informed sensitivity test:
QLP constructs a separate reference image within each TESS spacecraft orbit.
Each retained sector has one roughly three-day downlink gap and an aperture
reference-level jump. Splitting at observed gaps greater than 1.5 days gives
six arcs. Assign each arc its own offset, with optional global, sector or arc
slopes. These models use time and sector identity, without centroid/background
regressors. They were added after the initial fits and review, not preregistered.

J0830: A REPRODUCIBLE FIVE-WEEK MODULATION IN TESS

Nuisance model             | Free peak | Semiamplitude at Gaia | Fixed-period power
Six arc offsets            | 38.77 d   | 2.69%                 | 0.9620
Six offsets + global slope | 38.77 d   | 2.70%                 | 0.9628
Six offsets + sector slopes| 38.42 d   | 2.62%                 | 0.9582
Six offsets + arc slopes   | 39.68 d   | 3.13%                 | 0.8341

Power here means fractional residual sum-of-squares improvement over the same
nuisance-only model. It is not a probability or confidence level. The extra
arc slopes remove much more signal information; the simpler models carry the
main amplitude result. Free-peak digits describe the scan grid, not a precise
astrophysical period measurement or an uncertainty interval.

For the six-offset/sector-slope model, fit the stellar sine/cosine coefficients
on two sectors, freeze them and evaluate the third. Only that sector's nuisance
offsets/slopes are refitted. The held-out improvements are 0.919, 0.867 and
0.939 for S44, S45 and S46. Six offsets without slopes give 0.947, 0.953 and
0.955. Thus a common Gaia-period waveform explains the shapes of the separate
2021 sectors well, conditional on the stated calibration treatment.

The original centroid/background model also gives a 38.42-day
free peak and 2.53% semiamplitude. Target centroids are flux-weighted measurements
from the same pixels, not independent pointing telemetry. Importantly, the
reference-offset result above does not require those regressors. The unmodified
sector-offset-only curves favor other periods; treating the within-sector
reference changes matters. All unsuccessful model variants remain visible.

J0830 IN 2015: THE SAME DOMINANT PERIOD DOES NOT REAPPEAR

The original K2 SAP/PDC linear-baseline scans favor about 65–72 days, depending
on the quality mask. The fixed Gaia-period improvements are only 0.019–0.068
for SAP and 0.031–0.049 for PDC. Alternate baseline models change the peaks.
These are short, structured records, not a securely measured 70-day rotation.

K2SC explicitly separates position and time components. Following its product
documentation, restore time variability as flux + trtime - median(trtime).
Both restored SAP and PDC curves favor approximately 66.2 days with a linear
baseline, or the 100-day search boundary with a quadratic baseline. Neither
provides a stable dominant 38-day repeat. K2SC uses upstream release 7 rather
than the native product's release 31 and has different quality flags and
coverage. It is another reduction of the same epoch, not an independent
instrument or a common-cadence replication.

J0736: SHORTER AND LESS STABLE VARIABILITY

The reference-offset models peak at 20.23, 20.23, 20.53 and 19.39 days,
respectively. The original centroid/background fit peaks at 20.73 days.
This is near half of Gaia's 39.42-day period, but the waveform is imperfect
and several apparent structures depend on treatment of calibration changes.
At fixed Gaia period, the six-offset/sector-slope held-out scores are
0.518, -0.113 and 0.026; the original instrument model gives 0.539, 0.621 and
-0.390. A consistent 39.42-day wave is not established.

Two active longitudes can produce half-period modulation; an ellipsoidal
binary can also show two brightness maxima per orbit. Those are possibilities,
not classifications supplied by this light curve. J0736 remains useful for
follow-up, with a weaker photometric match to its Gaia candidate than J0830.

EXCURSIONS, HISTORICAL BEHAVIOR AND INTERPRETATION

The preliminary screen uses native corrected flux, a one-day rolling-median
baseline and local residual MAD within +/-0.5 days, requiring at least 20
accepted samples to estimate the noise. A candidate needs three consecutive
native cadence IDs above five local robust standard deviations, with no large
time gap and peak excess at least 0.2% for TESS or 0.5% for K2. Zero events
pass in all seven files. An independent implementation reproduces that result.
This is a bounded initial scan, not a flare-rate upper limit or proof that
either star is inactive; no injection completeness study was performed here.

The previous screening already recovered the published historical trends:
J0736's broad trough/recovery and J0830's sustained fading. It retained 800
and 1,379 standard-quality DASCH detections, respectively. Those screening
curves have not received J0754's same-exposure comparison-star replication.
Sector-normalized TESS flux cannot measure a century-scale change.

J0830's photometric timescale agreeing with a separate Gaia spectroscopic
candidate is useful evidence for the binary/activity investigation. Evolving
spots are compatible with a strong 2021 signal and a different 2015 waveform.
The light curves alone do not distinguish spots from orbital brightness
effects, validate a Gaia solution, localize all variability to the catalogue
star or explain the historical fade. Each epoch covers only about two proposed
cycles. A raw-pixel extraction with nearby comparison stars, followed by
independent seasonal photometry or radial velocities, is the clearest next test.

VERIFICATION AND REPRODUCIBILITY

An initial plan and original byte-hashed snapshot precede the period fits.
The protocol records later additions separately. Review corrected native
cadence bridging, the local-noise implementation and floating-point ties in
block-sign diagnostics. For the original J0830 instrument model, exact
enumeration of six 14-day blocks gives 2/64=0.03125. This is a conditional
sign-sensitivity result, not a calibrated false-alarm probability; it excludes
calibration-model selection and is not used to claim a discovery significance.
No null probability was retroactively assigned to the new arc-offset models.

All 205 component tests pass, including 17 sibling-analysis safeguards.
Native hashes, all fit/search outputs, failed requests, per-file accepted
counts and reproducible commands accompany this report. Four figures were
visually inspected. The independent review reproduced the numerical fits,
sector holdouts and corrected excursion null result. No email or manuscript
has been submitted.
