TESS: SIX AVAILABLE SECTOR IDENTIFIERS; ALL EIGHT LIGHT CURVES ALREADY SAVED

Current TESSCut metadata at the exact target position returns 44,45,46,71,72,
and 1751. It does not return 7 or 34. These absences mean no available calibrated
TESSCut coverage in those claimed sectors at this position, not astrophysical
nondetections. Sectors 44–46 in late 2021 and 71–72 in late 2023 are real coverage.

All eight local native FITS identify TIC 18878735: QLP 44/45/46/71/72 and
TESS-SPOC 71/72/1751. The corrected MAST timeseries query returns exactly the
same eight download URIs as the saved reconnaissance metadata. Therefore these
are confirmations of existing data, not new photometry. QLP and TESS-SPOC in
the same sector share photons and are alternative reductions.

Sector 1751 is a real special pointing, documented by NASA as dedicated
3I/ATLAS observations 15–22 January 2026, interrupting Sector 99. Its local FITS
dates agree. It is neither a virtual sector nor a stitched multi-sector token.
NASA reports about 4.25 science days within the seven-day interval, with safe-mode
gaps and special processing; it is not a normal 27 day sector. Official NASA
documentation is retained. The MIT page was found through web search, but its
direct-download TLS certificate chain failed; verification was not disabled.

One initial MAST query filtered obs_collection=TESS and returned no rows. The
target products actually belon g to HLSP. Correcting that filter recovers all 8.
The initial query null must not be used to claim absent TESS images. Existing
period/reduction limitations remain: this check supplies no new flare, rotation,
eclipse or 100 ppm precision result. No FFIs or new light curves were downloaded.
