Section3A: pressure scale height and radius
For4500K, mean molecular weight1.3 and logg=1.7 in CGS, g=.501187m/s2 and H_p=5.74253e7m=.0825432Rsun. This validates the attachment's corrected SI result. At an assumed1.2Msun, the radius implied by that same logg is25.6227Rsun and H/R=.322149%; five to ten heights would be1.61075–3.22149%. At.8Msun the implied radius is20.9208Rsun and H/R=.394550%; the same layer-count assumption instead gives1.97–3.95%.
The algebra does not establish the gravity, temperature, mean molecular weight, number of scale heights or geometrical extent of the CO-forming layer for this star. Local pressure scale height follows an ideal-gas hydrostatic model. Optical-depth intervals cannot be converted directly into a number of pressure heights without opacity/stratification. An atmosphere can depart from hydrostatic/isothermal assumptions. Neither a low reported spectroscopic logg nor disagreement with photometry proves non-LTE FeI over-ionization or a measured atmospheric radius change.
For comparison, using the cautioned Gaia GSPphot radius9.2474Rsun and temperature5205.776K, conditional masses.8–2.6Msun imply logg2.409–2.921 and H/R=.20175–.06208%. These are consistency cases, not accepted physical parameters. Gaia single-star atmosphere and FLAME giant-mass caveats remain.
The attachment's V=12.35,d=1668pc,Av=.15,BCv=-.5,T=4500K gives L≈45.7Lsun,R≈11.12Rsun by the standard equations. That arithmetic is reproducible but the asserted inputs are not a new radius measurement. The target-specific saved Tang2010 ASAS median is V=12.24, while the inverse saved Gaia parallax is≈1668pc; those measurements are asynchronous. Sensitivity cases with this historical V, assumedAv0–.5, BCv−.7 to−.3 and T4500/5200K give R≈7.46–15.08Rsun. This is an assumption grid, not a confidence interval. Prefer reconciling spectra, extinction, evolutionary modeling and variability before adopting a radius.

Section5B: partial covering and extinction slope
For a declared covering fraction f, tau_lambda=-ln[1-(1-10^(-.4*Delta_m_lambda))/f] is valid only when f exceeds every supplied flux loss. The minimum optical loss here is.2497604, not the loss from V=.35. A monochromatic tau∝lambda^(-alpha) curve fitted to actual g=.312 at.48um and W1=.1175 at3.4um gives:
f=1.00: alpha=.49882, tau_g=.28736; predicts R=.26821,I=.24182,W2=.10105mag.
f=.50: alpha=.56369, tau_g=.69219; predicts R=.27118,I=.24560,W2=.10003mag.
f=.30: alpha=.74158, tau_g=1.78698; predicts R=.28096,I=.25833,W2=.09676mag.
f=.26: alpha=.95190, tau_g=3.23442; predicts R=.29364,I=.27593,W2=.09234mag.
Thus alpha depends on covering and on the actual optical response. The attachment's V-based.61–.69 values do not transfer to g data. These examples alone do not identify a grain radius, universal optical-to-IR law or dust composition; no unique grain-size threshold follows from alpha. Optical saturation under partial covering can produce grey-looking optical fades even with ordinary wavelength-dependent opacity.
The existing passband-integrated Draine partial-covering hypotheses, extended without changing their old fits, predict R≈.31174–.31184,I≈.31066–.31100,W2≈.07028–.07503mag among their per-nuisance best fits. The nominal model has covering≈.24976 and high optical depth, and therefore nearly equal optical fades. The W2 mismatch and broader prior g/W1 tolerance envelopes must remain visible. A power-law screen and a saturated Draine screen are different hypotheses; their predictions must not be merged into a single dust prediction.

R/I forecasting and identification limit
At the same training rule, temperature-plus-scale best fits predict R=.1996–.2932,I=.1092–.2288; two-component occultation R=.2105–.2997,I=.1538–.2368; the three compatible fixed-area cooling cases R=.2240–.2566,I=.1614–.1684. A single grey multiplicative dimming and the tested uniform Draine screens cannot match both actual g and W1 within.02mag in this sampled grid. This rejects those restricted sampled forms, not every temperature/opacity geometry.
R−I changes for best-fit spot hypotheses span.0224–.1325mag, compared with.00084–.00108 for best-fit saturated Draine partial-cover hypotheses. These ranges are planning examples only. They are not full allowed envelopes, confidence intervals, likelihood ratios or a strict R−I decision boundary; the prior full compatibility envelopes, observational errors, .02mag training tolerance, baseline atmosphere uncertainty and broader model families must enter a joint refit. No new observed R/I state change has been substituted for these forecasts.
The nominal temperature-plus-scale fit implies4370.45K and geometric area ratio.93017 when fitting g/W1, then predicts W2=.12218 instead of.08875. An area ratio would correspond to a radius ratio only for an unobscured spherical emitter of the stated uniform temperature. Partial covering, multiple temperatures, dilution and calibration can produce the same scale. It is not a measured contraction.

Section5C: conditional binary geometry
The actual Gaia candidate gives f(M)=P*K1^3*(1-e2)^(3/2)/(2*pi*G)=.00458720Msun. For assumed primary masses.8,1.2,2.6Msun, edge-on minimum companion masses are.16191,.20881,.34108Msun. These are conditional on a real single-lined Keplerian orbit; they have not inherited a high-reliability orbit classification or fully propagated covariance. The K error is retained in the report and these point calculations are not hard companion bounds.
For a.5Msun companion, M1=.8/1.2Msun gives edge-on K=27.0937/22.6567km/s. Matching actual K requires i=23.3177/28.2517deg, rather than the attachment's17–21deg from an unsupported8km/s upper bound. Those particular.5Msun, radius-assumed cases are not aligned for simple stellar eclipses, but companions near the minimum mass can be edge-on; it is incorrect to exclude all eclipsing companions or all companion-related obscuration from this calculation.
With posterlogg1.7 radii, the.8 and1.2Msun primary Roche filling ratios are.9958 and1.0271. This shows tension among the assumptions, not demonstrated contact or mass transfer. With the cautioned Gaia9.2474Rsun radius the same configurations have filling.4402/.3707 and are detached. Eggleton's volume-radius approximation assumes the circular/synchronous Roche geometry; the candidate's small e does not validate every assumption.
The attachment's stated ellipsoidal coefficient15(15+u)(1+g)/[32(3-u)] is wrong for the standard leading fractional-flux SEMI-amplitude. The coefficient is.15(15+u)(1+g)/(3-u); the supplied expression is3.125times larger. For illustrative u=.8,g=.32, alpha=1.422. The leading term is A_ell=alpha*(M2/M1)*(R1/a)^3*sin2(i), relative to undiluted primary light, at twice orbital frequency. It requires modest tidal distortion; near/contact cases cannot be treated as precise amplitudes. The magnitude peak-to-peak modulation is2.5log10[(1+A)/(1-A)], about2.1715A for small A, so semi-amplitude and full amplitude must be distinguished.
At actual candidate inclination and Gaia radius, the.5Msun companion examples give fractional flux semi-amplitudes.0008809/.0006422 for M1=.8/1.2Msun. These are conditional illustrations, not measured signals or detection limits; limb/gravity darkening, dilution, spin synchronization and Roche distortion are not fitted. Earlier failure to establish a coherent photometric waveform at the Gaia period does not independently eliminate these systems without response/recovery modeling. A few-day central orbital crossing is also not a direct explanation for a multi-year fade; broader/evolving obscuring structures are separate models.
