TESS result and bounded pixel follow-up — J0754

The new finding is a flare-like brightening close to J0754 on 23 December 2021.
This is a candidate, not a formal stellar-flare classification.

The original eight files are extracted light curves. A subsequent authorized
7x7 MAST TESSCut recovered calibrated FFI pixels (3.91 MB), not complete FFIs
or unprocessed detector data. The event window contains 16 quality-zero frames;
its guarded before/after sidebands contain 43/40 frames. The new pixel extraction
shows a rise and decay across many cadences and a peak core contrast near 2.5%.
This is not a single-cadence cosmic spike.

The positive difference image is compact at the target: its centroid is 0.177
pixel from the Gaia WCS position, approximately 3.7 arcsec at TESS sampling.
This is a descriptive positive-excess moment, not a calibrated positional
confidence interval. The stationary source-shape model gives 2.02% brightening;
adding first-derivative image-motion terms changes it to 2.016%. Across nine
posthoc guarded-sideband choices the scale is 1.90–2.14% and the position offset
0.168–0.182pixel. These ranges are methodological sensitivities, not errors.
The simple core aperture contrast of 2.24% uses a raw-difference numerator and
must not be treated as an event-plane-subtracted precision amplitude.

QLP’s selected 3 h residual mean is 1.21%; its small/large aperture diagnostics
are 1.24%/1.24%, and SAP has the same-sign excursion. Those use different
baselines from the pixel difference, so their percentages are not interchangeable.
Local flux/centroid correlations are modest and do not establish an artifact.
TESSCut POS_CORR columns are entirely zero, supplying no independent jitter
measurement. The difference does not show an obvious multi-pixel traversal
within the selected window. Unresolved blends, a near-aligned moving source,
PRF/background errors and calibration effects remain possible. No moving-object
identity, flare energy or stellar mechanism was inferred.

The broader TESS analysis strongly limits confident absence claims. QLP corrected
20 min robust scatter is about 0.00093–0.00106 in fractional flux; SPOC sectors 71/72
give 0.00172/0.00522, with residual lag1 correlations about 0.60/0.48. Matching QLP
and SPOC highpass curves correlate only 0.16/0.34. The fixed 35.92/17.96 day amplitudes
and low-frequency structure are reduction/baseline dependent. This is not a new
orbital rejection or a secure rotation measurement.

The observed-residual bootstrap resamples its own candidate events and often
sets its threshold equal to the observed maximum. No excursions above that
uncalibrated descriptive screen is NOT evidence that flares are absent. All 96
selected positive/negative candidates remain saved, including false positives.
4,608 native-time injections show sharply varying response with product, amplitude,
duration and gaps. The 1/3/6 h boxes do not test multi-day giant transits; a 1 day
highpass suppresses the separate models’ 2.7–5.7 day transits. No universal 0.002 mag
transit/radius bound, 100 ppm precision or bolometric flare-energy threshold follows.
Signed observed equivalent durations are reported without a bolometric conversion.

Results.json, report.txt, the four rendered figures, native pixel ZIP/FITS,
per-frame/pixel maps and sensitivity JSONs are retained. The original protocol
and two chronological amendments remain separate. Final source and pixel/main
result hashes agree. All 114 current component tests pass, including 15 TESS
tests. The two assigned maintained files are saved and uncommitted for parent
review. No push or publication occurred.
