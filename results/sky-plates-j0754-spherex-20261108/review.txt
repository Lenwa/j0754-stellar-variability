Overall assessment
The load-bearing flux units, ePSF/native-pixel normalization, inverse-variance fit, cutout-to-detector mapping and matched QR2 wavelength provenance are sound after the SIP isolation repair. No factor-of25/100 error, detector-axis reversal or wrong cutout wavelength was found. The extraction supports a real, high-S/N recovery-era optical/near-IR continuum. It does not support precision seasonal variability or a statistically established small infrared excess from covariance errors alone.

P2: Refine the centroid-sensitivity diagnostic before describing it as a stability check
At review, extract_spherex.py lines80–87 tested only offsets(-.3,0,+.3)px. All182 primary rows choose zero on that grid, but this does not mean the fixed centroid is optimal or unbiased. An independent grid±.1px with step.02px on the worst accepted fit per detector finds best shifts .02–.06px and amplitude changes .19–1.99%. Example D2 2025W43_1A_0130_2D2: shift(-.04,-.04)px, flux+1.99%, chi2 decreases90%. D3 example shift(-.04,-.02)px, flux+.80%, chi2 decreases77%. These changes are comparable to tentative seasonal differences. Keep the predeclared fixed-position primary if desired, but refine this diagnostic and report the amplitude sensitivity. A fitted centroid would be a different estimator and should be labeled. Details: calibration/review_fine_centroid_sensitivity.json.

P2: Treat local errors as conditional covariance errors, not total measurement accuracy
spherex_photometry.py covariance inversion is correct for the stated fixed-template, fixed-position, independent-pixel linear model, including marginalization over the fitted plane. The model inadequacy is evident in actual residuals: median reduced chi2=6.99 over182 accepted rows. Detector medians D1–D6=11.89,23.00,12.22,8.79,1.97,1.74. Formal median fractional errors D1–D4 are only.31–.42%; residuals/centroid sensitivity and independent-method differences are larger. A threshold fit_ql<5 is a permissive screening rule, not a validation of a noise-limited fit. Reduced-chi2 error inflation can be a sensitivity analysis, but does not establish a calibrated correlated-systematic model. Plot labels should say formal/conditional errors or explicitly include PSF/astrometry/background limitations, not only calibration uncertainty.
The native table independently shows flux_err=sqrt(flux_err_tractor^2+(.03*flux)^2) for every220 matched rows, to saved precision. The3% term is an empirically identified property of the returned table, not a confirmed interpretation of all systematic covariance or an independent3% draw per exposure. Averaging many points cannot automatically reduce a common calibration term by sqrt(N).

Seasonal inference
Raw detector medians are invalid seasonal comparisons because the LVF samples different wavelengths. Within each detector, a post-hoc common polynomial in log-wavelength plus a seasonal log-amplitude gives2026/2025 approximately1.038(D1),1.017–1.018(D2),1.019–1.028(D3),1.019–1.020(D4),1.024–1.025(D5),1.018–1.020(D6) over polynomial orders1–3. These are descriptive sensitivity checks only: residual scatter is about2–5%, D2/D3 spectra have stellar structure a polynomial cannot represent, and detector-zone positions differ substantially between seasons. Within-season observations span days, especially April2026, so the assembled spectra are not instantaneous. No significance or causal inference should be assigned to these small scales. Use common physical spectra convolved with matched responses, shared detector/calibration nuisance parameters, and actual observation times for a stronger comparison.

Static photosphere check
Reviewed compare_spherex.py and the rendered photosphere_check.png. The F_lambda to F_nu shape conversion is correct; free normalization makes absolute stellar surface-flux units irrelevant to the shape test. Draine opacity normalization at.55um is consistent with A_V scaling. Equal log-flux fitting avoids treating the very small conditional local errors as calibrated likelihood weights. The code uses only.75–2.4um to select a reddened CK04 photosphere and then inspects longer wavelengths. It appropriately labels rectangular approximate passbands, retrospective/model-grid selection, degeneracy and the shared-pixel relationship of local/native reductions.
The existing best-fit cases show broad2.45–5um ratios around unity, with medians approximately.97–1.04 in the first two IR intervals and1.01–1.09 at4.4–5um. In particular, April2026 has a coherent roughly9%4.4–5um residual relative to those selected fits. It must remain visible as an unresolved residual, not be erased by saying there is no excess. Stellar molecular features, atmosphere/grid degeneracy, actual response weighting, calibration and method differences can matter at that scale. The proposed sampled envelopes are useful for displaying model sensitivity; their tolerance is not a confidence region.
The statement "A reddened stellar photosphere fitted below2.4um already accounts for most of the observed2.45–5um continuum; no dominant warm infrared emission component is required by this exploratory check" is supported with the stated qualifications. It is an existence/adequacy statement, not a dust nondetection or bound. It does not rule out a smaller warm component, cooler dust beyond5um, cold/opaque occulting material, or an emitting/absorbing mixture. It does not distinguish intrinsic variability from obscuration or establish the cause of the historical dimming. Do not quote best CK04 grid parameters as measured temperature/gravity/extinction, and do not treat this recovery-era static SED as the faint-versus-bright-state color change.
