J0754 RADIO AND RECOVERED-UV CHECK
10 November 2026, America/New_York

RADIO RESULT: NO DETECTION, NO SIGNIFICANT CHANGE
J0754 is not detected in any of the three recovered VLASS Quick Look images.
They are all nominally3GHz. A fixed-position restoring-beam fit gives:

UTC date       Forced flux density     Empirical1sigma     Approx3sigma bound
2019-04-15       -0.241mJy                0.144mJy              <0.432mJy
2021-11-20       +0.108mJy                0.146mJy              <0.545mJy
2024-06-01       -0.127mJy                0.127mJy              <0.382mJy

Negative fitted values are ordinary noise fluctuations around zero. They are
retained to avoid upward bias. The most positive epoch is only0.74sigma. No
nearby pixel within5arcsec provides a5sigma detection either. Allowing a
1arcsec position shift does not reveal a convincing source.

The three forced fluxes are consistent with a constant: chi-square3.03 for
2degrees of freedom, nominalp0.219. They are also consistent with zero flux
(nominalp0.226). These are descriptive noise-model checks, not calibrated
false-alarm probabilities. The images cannot establish whether the star is
faint and steady or has radio bursts outside these sparse observing epochs.
Radio nondetection does not negate the optical flare/activity evidence.

RADIO METHOD AND LIMITS
The actual FITS units are Jy/beam. Each image is fitted at the fixed Gaia
position using its own elliptical Gaussian restoring beam plus a local plane
within8arcsec. Pixel positions are converted to ICRS before fitting; an extra
equinox attribute in the WCS coordinate wrapper was explicitly removed after
the physical frame conversion. This avoids a strict-frame mismatch without
changing the sky coordinates.

Noise is measured with identical fits at an annulus of blank positions10-24
arcsec away, spaced3arcsec apart. Their robust scatter is the quoted error;
the median blank offset is subtracted. This includes correlated image pixels
instead of treating every pixel as an independent measurement. Blank placements
overlap and share a finite field, so the noise estimate has uncertainty. Pixel
RMS values are also retained as a cross-check.

The approximate bounds are max(0,forced flux)+3times the blank-fit scatter for
an unresolved source. They are image-level limits, not exact confidence bounds
on true physical flux. NRAO describes Quick Look flux-density uncertainty of
about15% for typical high-S/N detections and position accuracy about0.5arcsec.
No precise faint-source flux-bias correction is inferred. The restored beams
differ between epochs; the common point-source model accounts for their shapes.
No radio variability detection survives these basic noise checks.

SWIFT UVW1: A REAL EXPOSURE-EDGE PROBLEM
The two sky-image extensions begin at15:33:15 and17:09:37UTC on2December2009.
The first has2519s in the field-exposure header, but only8.44s at the star's
central pixel. Across a5arcsec aperture the exposure ranges from0 to2370s,
with median9.35s: the source straddles a sharp detector-coverage edge. A single
aperture exposure cannot calibrate that footprint without PSF/exposure modelling.
The counts are therefore not converted to a rate for epoch comparison.

The second extension has a uniform2390.33s across that aperture and a clear
source. It contains about17905 recorded aperture counts, an estimated459
background counts, and an uncorrected net rate about7.30counts/s. This is a
recorded count rate, not a new calibrated UV magnitude: coincidence loss,
aperture correction, large-scale sensitivity and the UVW1 optical red tail
still matter. Image resampling also correlates count errors.

The exposure map explains why direct first/second counts would suggest a
spectacular apparent brightening. There is no defensible two-image UV-flare
claim here. The target's existing UVOT catalogue flag128 (multiple exposure
values in the source aperture) now has a concrete pixel-level explanation.

GALEX: SIMILAR VISIT MEANS, MODEST RAW FLUCTUATIONS
The460 retrieved near-UV photons split into260 over97 nominal aspect seconds
and200 over82s: raw6arcsec-aperture rates2.68 and2.44counts/s. The exact
conditional between-visit binomial check givesp0.326, consistent with the same
raw average rate. The visits are only about1.65h apart on2February2006.

The predeclared20s bins are not perfectly flat. A raw Poisson deviance20.97
for9degrees of freedom gives nominalp0.0128. That is a possible fluctuation
to retain, not a calibrated physical variability claim. The second run ends
with a2s partial bin. Different binning, endpoint exposure, aperture losses,
detector response, background and photon quality can matter in such a short
series. There is no established flare morphology in these ten noisy bins.

Both spacecraft files have status_flag0, nominal NUV high voltage and no filter
wheel motion. The t_dead_nuv telemetry column is stable near0.164, ranging
roughly0.161-0.166; its calibration was not applied as a live-time correction.
Detector positions and all bin counts are retained. No bolometric UV energy or
strong upper limit follows from three minutes of uncalibrated aperture photons.

eROSITA: POSITIONALLY PLAUSIBLE, NOT A UNIQUE IDENTIFICATION
The DR2 source remains8.35arcsec from J0754, compared with the documented
catalogue1sigma positional-error field5.82arcsec (ratio1.43). Its source-quality
flags for spurious detections, optical contamination and missing errors are
not raised. This supports keeping the association as a plausible candidate.

However, UVOTSSC1 J075445.0+164128 lies9.38arcsec from the X-ray centroid,
nearly as close as the bright star. It has UVW1 Vega20.39 and quality flag0.
That catalogue object has not been independently identified or verified as an
X-ray emitter. Its presence means proximity alone does not uniquely identify
the eROSITA source. The brighter J0754 is still a viable counterpart, and the
previous Swift association is separate evidence. No claim of a newly confirmed
X-ray flare or exact source association is made from this comparison.

WHAT CHANGED
The radio images give useful nondetections and approximate sensitivity bounds.
Swift's misleading exposure comparison is now resolved at the pixel level.
GALEX supplies a short raw count series with consistent visit means and a weak
within-visit variability lead. eROSITA remains a candidate association requiring
counterpart work. None of these nulls or limitations erases the TESS events.
