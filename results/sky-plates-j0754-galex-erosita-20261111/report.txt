J0754: GALEX calibration and released eROSITA counterpart
11 November 2026

FINDINGS

eROSITA: the survey team's released Gaia/NWAY catalogue selects J0754 itself,
Gaia DR3 667401913089980544, as the best counterpart to
3eRASS J075445.3+164136 (UID 311707200169). Its p_any is 0.918834;
p_i is 0.99999994, and match_flag is 1. The offset is 8.34644 arcsec.
This materially strengthens the independent X-ray association beyond the
positional match in our previous report.

p_any is the probability that a counterpart exists in the input Gaia catalogue;
p_i is its relative probability among that catalogue's candidate counterparts.
The nearly-unity p_i is not a 99.999994% unconditional identification. The
X-ray-emitter Gaia prior is 0.924435, distinct from the output probability.
Only one row for this X-ray UID occurs in the complete released Gaia table.
The faint UVOT source 9.38 arcsec from the X-ray position remains outside this
Gaia-only comparison; it is not thereby proven spurious. J0754 is nevertheless
the preferred, physically supported identification in this independent release.
DR2 is catalogue-only, so it cannot tell us whether eROSITA caught a flare.
The detection supplies evidence for X-ray activity, not a flare light curve.

GALEX: modest short-timescale fluctuations remain after correcting background,
flat response, time-dependent sensitivity, deadtime and shutter coverage.
The detector corrections do not explain away the 20-second fluctuations.
They agree across 6, 9 and 12 arcsec apertures; variable aperture loss alone
does not explain the shape. No clear fast-rise/exponential-decay event appears.

The evidence for variability depends strongly on temporal binning. Approximate
constant-source Poisson-reference probabilities (100,000 simulations each):

aperture       20-second bins    30-second bins    40-second bins
6 arcsec       0.0111            0.5848            0.4152
9 arcsec       0.0176            0.4471            0.2730
12 arcsec      0.0107            0.4672            0.2188

These are uncorrected for the multiple, correlated analysis choices. They are
conditional on response tracks estimated from detected photon positions.
Conclusion: a weak UV variability hint, insufficient for a new flare claim.
The two very short visits (97 and 82 nominal seconds, separated by ~1.65 hr)
cannot establish an hours-long flare or a long-term activity cycle.

METHOD AND DATA

Retrieved 1,057 nominal NUV photons within 30 arcsec, including source photons
and a 20-30 arcsec background annulus. Reused the independently retrieved
spacecraft/aspect coverage. Source apertures are 6, 9 and 12 arcsec. Native
millisecond photon times are converted to seconds. Short trailing bins below
half the requested bin length are merged, avoiding the previous 2-second tail.

Flat coordinates and the NUV time-sensitivity polynomial follow gPhoton's
curvetools and galextools. Photons receive inverse-response weights; count
errors use a Poisson reference conditional on each bin's harmonic response.
Detector dead fractions follow gPhoton's empirical global-count model, using
both NUVPhotonsV flag=0 and NUVPhotonsNULLV. Global event counts in native
50-millisecond intervals measure open coverage directly, including partial
intervals at bin boundaries. The old two-argument shutter function is no longer
accepted by the server: its explicit ERROR responses are preserved but unused.
No inferred scalar was substituted for that failure.

No infinite-aperture correction is made: corrected count rates are aperture
rates, not an absolute UV luminosity. Background simulation includes both
source-aperture and annulus photon noise. Probabilities test a constant source
under the stated detector model; they do not identify the cause of a departure.

The eROSITA Gaia catalogue is 855,031,704 compressed bytes. Every binary-table
row was streamed and checked by UID, retaining the target row and original
header only. The public FITS repeats the name GDR3_source_id; the second copy
is explicitly renamed GDR3_source_id_duplicate in the retained ECSV. The
original field order/header is saved. Null purity/completeness fields remain
null; they are not additional probability measurements.

REPRODUCTION

python -B tools/sky-plates/fetch_galex_erosita_followup.py galex
python -B tools/sky-plates/fetch_galex_erosita_followup.py erosita
python -B tools/sky-plates/analyze_galex_erosita_followup.py
python -B -m unittest discover -s tools/sky-plates -p 'test_*.py'

Fetch commands reuse saved inputs. Analysis is offline. protocol.json precedes
the corrected analysis but follows earlier inspection of raw counts/positions.
galex_corrected.png shows aperture sensitivity and detector factors. Exact
counts, effective exposures, response factors and simulations are in results.json.
