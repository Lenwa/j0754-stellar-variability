Ari with Kyle — 8 November 2026

Outcome: several distinct spectral-change models remain compatible with the measured modern optical/infrared fading. The simplest uniform foreground screen and a purely wavelength-independent single-component fade do not fit both training bands within the declared tolerance. The stronger result is a set of testable color predictions, new historical photometry, a recovered mean spectrum, and confirmed post-2024 infrared image coverage. No unique cause or long recurrence period has been established.

Boundary/physical limits: some spot solutions reach the3500K cool-grid floor; colder spot spectra could change results. The fitted companion area ratios and covering fractions are spectral geometries, not a validated coeval binary or dynamically viable multi-year eclipse. A fitted geometric scale is not a radius measurement. No calibrated static GaiaXP fit was used to weight/prune these baseline assumptions. Model families with different grains/emission, atmospheric grids or mixtures remain open.

WHAT THIS CHANGES
The investigation now has competing quantitative predictions rather than a dust assumption, real2002–2009photometry, usable mean optical spectral context, and a public2025–26IRspectral-image route. It has not identified a cause. The most useful immediate archive analysis is to extract calibrated SPHEREx source spectra with comparison/calibration checks and match them to actual contemporary optical brightness states. This can constrain near-IR structure and later recovery; it cannot recreate a missing2022spectrum. A new multiepoch optical spectrum is still needed for activity/temperature/line-shape/velocity diagnosis. No telescope booking, equipment purchase, account or contact was made.
