J0754: THREE ADDITIONAL SOURCE-CENTERED FLARE CANDIDATES
10 November 2026, America/New_York

Finding
The search recovers the previously studied 23 December 2021 event and finds
three additional source-centered, hour-scale brightenings in calibrated TESS
pixels. The new events are smaller than the original. They survive direct
aperture extraction, background removal and a spatial source/motion test.

Data and coverage
Eight saved QLP/TESS-SPOC lightcurve FITS represent six distinct intervals:
44,45,46,71,72 and1751. Quality-retained cadence intervals union to104.18 days;
alternate reductions of the same exposures are counted once. Using QLP for
the five long sectors plus the short latest interval gives102.31 days. These
are nominal accepted-cadence coverage, not continuous calendar monitoring or
an exposure-time/dead-time correction.

A fresh public MAST3-arcsecond cone search returns33 observation records and
the same eight extracted TESS products. TESSCut still returns the same six
intervals, so no new observing dates were found in that query. Five new full
7x7 calibrated pixel series were downloaded for44,45,71,72 and1751; the prior
Sector46 cutout was reused. New files total31.75MB uncompressed. Public query
responses, exact retrieval URLs and file hashes are retained. These pixels
are calibrated FFI cutouts, not raw uncalibrated detector images.

Search
Corrected lightcurves were searched at native cadence and in20-minute bins.
After a two-day running median, a candidate needs at least3 consecutive
points above3 robust adjacent-difference noise units and a peak of5 units.
Runs break at gaps over2.5 typical cadences. Positive and negative detections
are retained. These numbers screen candidates; correlated noise prevents
interpreting them as Gaussian detection significance.

The corrected-product search recovers November22 and November28, plus the
original event. Baselines of1,2 and3 days preserve their positive peaks.
November22 appears in SAP and alternate QLP apertures. November28 appears in
both providers' SAP lightcurves and SPOC corrected flux, but is substantially
attenuated by QLP correction. Alternate reductions reuse the same photons.

A separate direct-pixel search bypasses both providers' flattening and finds
the November16 candidate in addition to those three. It is away from quality
boundaries; it was missed by the corrected-product screen. This illustrates
why corrected-flux non-detections alone cannot exclude flares.

Spatial checks
For the four candidates, fixed event windows are compared with interpolated
before/after images. An outer-ring plane is removed from the difference map.
Each excess is compact and close to J0754's Gaia WCS position. Fitting a source
brightness change jointly with image-gradient motion terms changes the fitted
source scale by less than0.3% relative in all four cases. Telescope motion is
therefore not a sufficient explanation in this diagnostic.

Before/event/after quality-retained frames are43/11/43 for November16,
23/11/43 for November22,43/16/40 for December23, and129/78/129 for November28.
These counts correspond to the primary guard0.15d/side0.3d; see
pixel_followup.json for all variants.
The larger pre-event sidebands for November22 have too few accepted frames;
those failures are explicitly retained. Working sideband variants keep its
excess near0.19pixel. November16 is weaker and more baseline-sensitive:
offset0.09-0.33pixel, event-mean source scale0.27-0.57%. November28 remains
within0.05-0.20pixel across the sideband choices. The source-scale amplitudes
are averages over selected event windows and differ from peak amplitudes.

Rejected and unresolved excursions
The extracted-product screen returns11 product-level positive hits: the known
event, the two new hits and8 other detections. Most of those additional hits
occur near quality gaps/edges or lack a comparable SAP/alternate-reduction
signal. The direct pixel search returns9 hits, of which4 are the four events
above. Other direct-aperture hits include gap-boundary excursions, broad
background/position-linked structures, displaced or nonpositive source-shape
contrasts, and one event lacking usable sidebands. All hits are retained in
the JSON/figures. None of these five is promoted to a new stellar flare here.

Sensitivity
1296 synthetic FRED-like profiles were injected into the quality-retained
native extracted lightcurves before binning and median removal:24 uniform
times x6 nonduplicated preferred products x3 widths x3 amplitudes. Detection
uses the native OR20-minute screen. The observed robust noise estimate is held
fixed for paired recovery trials; the baseline is recomputed near each added
event. These tests measure this search's response in already-reduced data.
They do not correct for signal removed by upstream QLP/SPOC processing.

For4.6-hour,2.5%-peak profiles, all115 trials with at least80% of the profile
sampled were recovered;129/144 were recovered including trials affected by
gaps. For0.5-hour,0.5%-peak profiles, only8/120 well-sampled trials recovered.
The search can find original-sized events when sampled but misses many small,
short events. No formal flare-frequency distribution or absence limit follows.

Interpretation and next step
We now have four spatially consistent flare-like events rather than a single
isolated excursion. That strengthens the repeated magnetic-activity picture.
November16 has the least robust baseline/localization of the four. The data
support three additional candidates; a calibrated PRF/centroid uncertainty
analysis and an unresolved-neighbor/moving-source check would strengthen their
classification. No new bolometric energies are assigned in this search, and
these events do not by themselves settle the cause of the years-long dimming.
